Earth



Earth Day, celebrated annually on April 22, is a global event to raise awareness and encourage action for environmental protection. It originated in 1970 in the United States and has grown to include over 193 countries, with activities like tree planting, clean-up drives, and educational campaigns focusing on issues like climate change, pollution, and conservation. The day serves as a reminder of our planet's fragility and the need for sustainable practices to preserve it for future generations.
Origin: Earth Day was first observed in 1970 as a response to a growing concern about environmental degradation, particularly following an oil spill in Santa Barbara, California.
Global reach: What began in the US has become an international event, celebrated by over 193 countries to promote environmental preservation.
Activities: On Earth Day, people participate in various activities to support the environment, such as planting trees, cleaning up litter, recycling, and attending educational events.
Purpose: The day aims to draw attention to critical environmental issues such as global warming, pollution, deforestation, and rising sea levels, and to inspire individuals and governments to take action and adopt more sustainable practices. Individual contribution: The day emphasizes that everyone, regardless of age or location, can contribute to a healthier planet through small actions like saving water and energy, reducing waste, and using less plastic.
